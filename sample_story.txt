Chapter 1: The Lantern Under Bellwater Bridge

Nila had crossed Bellwater Bridge every morning since she was old enough to carry bread to the market, but she had never seen the lantern burning beneath it. The river was low that summer, thin and green between the stones, and the light swung under the arch as if someone invisible had just passed by.

Her grandmother had warned her about river-lights. "If the water shows you a door," Grandmother Sera used to say, "walk away before it learns your name." Nila had laughed at the warning when she was younger, but the lantern was carved with the same crescent mark that hung above Sera's locked sewing room.

By noon, half the town had heard that something strange was glowing under Bellwater Bridge. Master Orrin from the clock shop called it reflected sunlight, though there was no sun. The baker said it was a fisherman's trick. Only Tomas, who knew every current in the river, refused to look over the railing.

That evening Nila returned with Sera's brass thimble in her pocket. She did not know why she had taken it, only that the thimble had warmed in her hand the moment she stepped onto the bridge. Below her, the lantern stopped swinging and the river gathered itself into the shape of a narrow black door.

When the door opened, Nila heard her grandmother's voice calling from the other side. It did not sound frightened. It sounded young. Nila held the brass thimble tight, whispered her own name before the river could ask for it, and climbed down toward the light.
